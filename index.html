UNESCO 0/45 · Jyotirlinga & Dham 0/15

Andhra Pradesh 0/1

  • Mallikarjuna Temple

    Andhra Pradesh · Pilgrimage

    A hilltop Jyotirlinga above the Krishna river gorge at Srisailam, one of the few shrines counted among both the Jyotirlingas and the Shakti Peethas.

    Wikipedia

Assam 0/3

  • Kaziranga National Park

    Assam · Natural · 1985

    Assam grassland and swampland reserve holding the world's largest population of one-horned rhinoceros, best explored by jeep or elephant-back safari.

    Wikipedia UNESCO

  • Manas Wildlife Sanctuary

    Assam · Natural · 1985

    Foothill forest on the Bhutan border sheltering tigers, elephants and the rare golden langur — quieter and far less visited than Kaziranga.

    Wikipedia UNESCO

  • Moidams – the Mound-Burial System of the Ahom Dynasty

    Assam · Cultural · 2024

    Earthen burial mounds of the Ahom dynasty at Charaideo, raised over six centuries and sometimes called Assam's own pyramids.

    Wikipedia UNESCO

Bihar 0/2

  • Archaeological Site of Nalanda Mahavihara at Nalanda, Bihar

    Bihar · Cultural · 2016

    Ruins of the ancient world's largest residential university, teaching Buddhist philosophy to students from across Asia from the 5th to 12th centuries.

    Wikipedia UNESCO

  • Mahabodhi Temple Complex at Bodh Gaya

    Bihar · Cultural · 2002

    The site where the Buddha is believed to have attained enlightenment, under a descendant of the original Bodhi tree — still an active pilgrimage site.

    Wikipedia UNESCO

Chandigarh 0/1

  • The Architectural Work of Le Corbusier

    Chandigarh · Cultural · 2016

    The Capitol Complex — Chandigarh's Secretariat, Assembly and High Court — designed by Le Corbusier as the centrepiece of India's first planned city.

    Wikipedia UNESCO

Delhi 0/3

  • Humayun's Tomb

    Delhi · Cultural · 1993

    A garden tomb built for the Mughal emperor Humayun in the 1560s, and the direct architectural template for the Taj Mahal decades later.

    Wikipedia UNESCO

  • Qutb Minar and its Monuments

    Delhi · Cultural · 1993

    A 73-metre tapering minaret begun in 1193 to mark the start of Delhi's Sultanate era, standing beside an even older iron pillar.

    Wikipedia UNESCO

  • Red Fort

    Delhi · Cultural · 2007

    Shah Jahan's red-sandstone citadel and seat of Mughal power for two centuries — every Independence Day, the Prime Minister addresses the nation from its ramparts.

    Wikipedia UNESCO

Goa 0/1

  • Churches and Convents of Goa

    Goa · Cultural · 1986

    16th–17th century Portuguese churches in Old Goa, including the Basilica of Bom Jesus, which holds the relics of St. Francis Xavier.

    Wikipedia UNESCO

Gujarat 0/7

  • Champaner-Pavagadh Archaeological Park

    Gujarat · Cultural · 2004

    An abandoned 16th-century sultanate capital at the foot of Pavagadh hill, blending Hindu and Islamic architecture across mosques, tombs and stepwells.

    Wikipedia UNESCO

  • Dholavira: a Harappan City

    Gujarat · Cultural · 2021

    A 5,000-year-old Indus Valley city in the Rann of Kutch, with stone reservoirs and a stadium-sized ceremonial ground still visible today.

    Wikipedia UNESCO

  • Dwarkadhish Temple

    Gujarat · Pilgrimage

    Krishna's Char Dham temple in his legendary capital of Dwarka, its five-storey spire visible from far along the Gujarat coast.

    Wikipedia

  • Historic City of Ahmadabad

    Gujarat · Cultural · 2017

    A 15th-century walled city of carved-wood havelis and stepped pols, founded by Sultan Ahmad Shah on the banks of the Sabarmati.

    Wikipedia UNESCO

  • Nageshwar Jyotirlinga

    Gujarat · Pilgrimage

    A Jyotirlinga near Dwarka marked by a giant Shiva statue, one of three shrines across India that claim the name Nageshwar.

    Wikipedia

  • Rani-ki-Vav (the Queen's Stepwell) at Patan, Gujarat

    Gujarat · Cultural · 2014

    An 11th-century stepwell built as a memorial by a Solanki queen, its seven descending levels carved with over 500 sculptures.

    Wikipedia UNESCO

  • Somnath Temple

    Gujarat · Pilgrimage

    The first of the twelve Jyotirlingas, on Gujarat's Arabian Sea coast — rebuilt several times after historic raids, its current shikhara dates to 1951.

    Wikipedia

Himachal Pradesh 0/1

  • Great Himalayan National Park Conservation Area

    Himachal Pradesh · Natural · 2014

    A steep Himachal wilderness of conifer forest and alpine pasture, home to snow leopards and blue sheep, reached mainly on foot.

    Wikipedia UNESCO

Jharkhand 0/1

  • Baidyanath Temple

    Jharkhand · Pilgrimage

    A Jyotirlinga at Deoghar also revered as one of the Shakti Peethas, drawing pilgrims who carry Ganga water on foot from Sultanganj.

    Wikipedia

Karnataka 0/3

  • Group of Monuments at Hampi

    Karnataka · Cultural · 1986

    The boulder-strewn ruins of Vijayanagara's capital — temples, bazaars and a stone chariot spread across a landscape that looks otherworldly at dawn.

    Wikipedia UNESCO

  • Group of Monuments at Pattadakal

    Karnataka · Cultural · 1987

    A cluster of 7th–8th century Chalukya temples blending northern and southern Indian temple styles side by side on the Malaprabha river.

    Wikipedia UNESCO

  • Sacred Ensembles of the Hoysalas

    Karnataka · Cultural · 2023

    Star-shaped 12th–13th century temples at Belur, Halebidu and Somanathapura, carved by Hoysala sculptors in soapstone fine enough to resemble ivory.

    Wikipedia UNESCO

Kerala 0/1

  • Western Ghats

    Kerala · Natural · 2012

    A mountain range older than the Himalayas, running down India's west coast — one of the world's biodiversity hotspots across 39 protected pockets.

    Wikipedia UNESCO

Madhya Pradesh 0/5

  • Buddhist Monuments at Sanchi

    Madhya Pradesh · Cultural · 1989

    India's best-preserved Buddhist stupa complex, founded by Ashoka in the 3rd century BCE, with elaborately carved gateways added later by the Sungas.

    Wikipedia UNESCO

  • Khajuraho Group of Monuments

    Madhya Pradesh · Cultural · 1986

    Chandela-dynasty temples from the 10th–11th centuries, celebrated for intricately carved sculpture — only a fraction of the original 85 temples survive.

    Wikipedia UNESCO

  • Mahakaleshwar Temple

    Madhya Pradesh · Pilgrimage

    Ujjain's Jyotirlinga, unusual for facing south, known for its pre-dawn Bhasma Aarti where the deity is ritually bathed in sacred ash.

    Wikipedia

  • Omkareshwar Temple

    Madhya Pradesh · Pilgrimage

    A Jyotirlinga on a Narmada river island shaped like the sacred Om symbol, reached by a footbridge from the mainland ghats.

    Wikipedia

  • Rock Shelters of Bhimbetka

    Madhya Pradesh · Cultural · 2003

    Sandstone rock shelters with paintings spanning the Mesolithic to medieval periods — among the earliest evidence of human life in India.

    Wikipedia UNESCO

Maharashtra 0/9

  • Ajanta Caves

    Maharashtra · Cultural · 1983

    30 rock-cut Buddhist caves in a horseshoe gorge, carved between the 2nd century BCE and 6th century CE, famous for painted murals of the Jataka tales.

    Wikipedia UNESCO

  • Bhimashankar Temple

    Maharashtra · Pilgrimage

    A Jyotirlinga tucked into a forested wildlife sanctuary in the Sahyadri hills, and the source of the Bhima river.

    Wikipedia

  • Chhatrapati Shivaji Terminus (formerly Victoria Terminus)

    Maharashtra · Cultural · 2004

    A Victorian Gothic railway station in daily use, its spires and turrets modelled on London's St Pancras and still Mumbai's busiest terminus.

    Wikipedia UNESCO

  • Elephanta Caves

    Maharashtra · Cultural · 1987

    A short ferry ride from Mumbai's Gateway of India to rock-cut Shiva shrines, centred on a monumental three-faced Sadashiva sculpture.

    Wikipedia UNESCO

  • Ellora Caves

    Maharashtra · Cultural · 1983

    34 caves cut side by side — Buddhist, Hindu and Jain — crowned by the Kailasa Temple, carved top-down from a single basalt cliff.

    Wikipedia UNESCO

  • Grishneshwar Temple

    Maharashtra · Pilgrimage

    The last of the twelve Jyotirlingas, rebuilt in the 18th century barely a kilometre from the Ellora Caves.

    Wikipedia

  • Maratha Military Landscapes of India

    Maharashtra · Cultural · 2025

    Twelve hill, forest and coastal forts built by the Maratha empire, headlined by Shivaji's mountain stronghold at Raigad.

    Wikipedia UNESCO

  • Trimbakeshwar Shiva Temple

    Maharashtra · Pilgrimage

    A black-stone Jyotirlinga near Nashik where the Godavari river is believed to originate, its inner sanctum holding three small lingas for the Trimurti.

    Wikipedia

  • Victorian Gothic and Art Deco Ensembles of Mumbai

    Maharashtra · Cultural · 2018

    Two adjoining South Mumbai precincts — Gothic Revival civic buildings around Oval Maidan facing an Art Deco skyline along Marine Drive.

    Wikipedia UNESCO

Odisha 0/2

  • Jagannath Temple, Puri

    Odisha · Pilgrimage

    The Char Dham temple on Odisha's coast, famous for its annual Rath Yatra, when the deities are pulled through the streets on giant wooden chariots.

    Wikipedia

  • Sun Temple, Konark

    Odisha · Cultural · 1984

    A 13th-century temple built as a colossal stone chariot for the sun god, its wheels carved as working sundials, half-ruined but still monumental.

    Wikipedia UNESCO

Rajasthan 0/4

  • Hill Forts of Rajasthan

    Rajasthan · Cultural · 2013

    Six hilltop Rajput forts, including Chittorgarh and Kumbhalgarh, built between the 5th and 18th centuries to guard the kingdoms below.

    Wikipedia UNESCO

  • Jaipur City, Rajasthan

    Rajasthan · Cultural · 2019

    Rajasthan's pink-washed walled capital, planned in 1727 on a precise grid of nine blocks around the City Palace and Hawa Mahal.

    Wikipedia UNESCO

  • Keoladeo National Park

    Rajasthan · Natural · 1985

    A former royal duck-hunting marsh near Bharatpur, now a wetland sanctuary drawing thousands of migratory birds every winter.

    Wikipedia UNESCO

  • The Jantar Mantar, Jaipur

    Rajasthan · Cultural · 2010

    An 18th-century open-air observatory of stone astronomical instruments built by Maharaja Jai Singh II, including the world's largest stone sundial.

    Wikipedia UNESCO

Sikkim 0/1

  • Khangchendzonga National Park

    Sikkim · Mixed · 2016

    A Sikkim reserve wrapped around the world's third-highest peak, sacred to local communities and home to red pandas and snow leopards.

    Wikipedia UNESCO

Tamil Nadu 0/3

  • Great Living Chola Temples

    Tamil Nadu · Cultural · 1987

    Three living Shiva temples built over three centuries by Chola kings, headlined by the towering 11th-century Brihadeeswarar Temple at Thanjavur.

    Wikipedia UNESCO

  • Group of Monuments at Mahabalipuram

    Tamil Nadu · Cultural · 1984

    Pallava-era rock-cut shrines and the Shore Temple facing the Bay of Bengal, alongside the giant open-air relief known as Arjuna's Penance.

    Wikipedia UNESCO

  • Ramanathaswamy Temple

    Tamil Nadu · Pilgrimage

    A Jyotirlinga and Char Dham site on an island linked to the Ramayana, famed for a pillared corridor among the longest of any Hindu temple.

    Wikipedia

Telangana 0/1

  • Kakatiya Rudreshwara (Ramappa) Temple, Telangana

    Telangana · Cultural · 2021

    A 13th-century Kakatiya temple with a roof said to be light enough to float on water, and sandstone carving famed for its detail.

    Wikipedia UNESCO

Uttar Pradesh 0/5

  • Agra Fort

    Uttar Pradesh · Cultural · 1983

    Mughal red-sandstone fortress on the Yamuna, built by Akbar and later the site of Shah Jahan's confinement, with a clear view of the Taj.

    Wikipedia UNESCO

  • Ancient Buddhist Site of Sarnath

    Uttar Pradesh · Cultural · 2026

    The deer park where the Buddha gave his first sermon after enlightenment, marked today by the Ashoka pillar and the great Dhamek Stupa.

    Wikipedia UNESCO

  • Fatehpur Sikri

    Uttar Pradesh · Cultural · 1986

    Akbar's short-lived red-sandstone capital, abandoned within 15 years for lack of water — its palaces and the towering Buland Darwaza remain intact.

    Wikipedia UNESCO

  • Kashi Vishwanath Temple

    Uttar Pradesh · Pilgrimage

    Varanasi's Jyotirlinga on the Ganga's ghats, rebuilt in its present form in 1780 and now linked to the river by a broad new corridor.

    Wikipedia

  • Taj Mahal

    Uttar Pradesh · Cultural · 1983

    Shah Jahan's white-marble mausoleum for Mumtaz Mahal, finished in 1653 and inlaid with semi-precious stone — best seen at sunrise, before the crowds.

    Wikipedia UNESCO

Uttarakhand 0/3

  • Badrinath Temple

    Uttarakhand · Pilgrimage

    The Himalayan Char Dham dedicated to Vishnu, open only from spring to autumn before Garhwal's snows close the valley.

    Wikipedia

  • Kedarnath Temple

    Uttarakhand · Pilgrimage

    A stone Shiva temple at 3,583m in the Garhwal Himalaya, reachable only on foot or by helicopter, open to pilgrims a few months each year.

    Wikipedia

  • Nanda Devi and Valley of Flowers National Parks

    Uttarakhand · Natural · 1988

    A high-Himalayan wilderness of alpine meadows and snow peaks around Nanda Devi, open to trekkers only through the short monsoon bloom.

    Wikipedia UNESCO

West Bengal 0/3

  • Mountain Railways of India

    West Bengal · Cultural · 1999

    Three narrow-gauge Himalayan lines — Darjeeling, Nilgiri and Kalka–Shimla — still climbing steep grades on steam and diesel toy trains.

    Wikipedia UNESCO

  • Santiniketan

    West Bengal · Cultural · 2023

    The university town Rabindranath Tagore built around open-air classrooms and rural craft, still run on the Visva-Bharati philosophy he founded in 1921.

    Wikipedia UNESCO

  • Sundarbans National Park

    West Bengal · Natural · 1987

    The world's largest mangrove forest, shared with Bangladesh, explored by boat through tidal channels where the Bengal tiger still swims between islands.

    Wikipedia UNESCO

"Char Dham" here means the original four sites defined by Adi Shankaracharya — Badrinath, Dwarka, Puri and Rameswaram — not the Uttarakhand "Chota Char Dham" pilgrim circuit (Yamunotri, Gangotri, Kedarnath, Badrinath).
